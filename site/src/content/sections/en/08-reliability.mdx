A word that relies on ECC for a hard fault has nothing left for a soft error: one upset in its other 29
bits becomes an uncorrectable, detected error. The model counts single-bit upsets only and assumes
1000 FIT per megabit, because no soft-error measurement exists for this macro; the result scales with
that assumption.

The cost is linear in the number of ECC-reliant words, so the budget K is a hard ceiling on it. On the
same dies, the joint allocator uses far fewer ECC words than an ECC-first scheme at low defect counts, and
partitioned steering lowers the number further. The ECC-first baselines follow the published description
of EBISR in outline; they were not checked against the full paper.
