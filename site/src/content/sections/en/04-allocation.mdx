title: "How the repair is chosen"

The address space is split into 16 partitions of 16 words. For each partition the controller runs
March C- on the raw 32 bits and keeps a bitmap of the failing cells of that partition only. With two
spare columns there are at most 466 ways to choose which code bits they replace, so the hardware simply
tries them. For each choice a word with two or more remaining faulty bits must take a spare word, and a
word with exactly one is left to ECC unless a spare word is free. This gives, per partition, the smallest
number of ECC-reliant words for every possible share of the spare words.

A small dynamic programme then divides the spare words among the partitions so that the total number of
ECC-reliant words is minimal. The memory is repairable if that total does not exceed the budget K. A
second pass programs the steering table and the spare words; a third pass runs March C- on the whole
memory through the repaired datapath.

Faults in the spare columns themselves are part of the bitmap, so a bad spare cell is never chosen where
it is needed. The fault store is the bitmap of one partition, so it cannot overflow.
